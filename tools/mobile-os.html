    Mobile OS

    last updated 2026-11-09 · 4 recommendations · what changed

    Your phone is the most personal computer you own and the most chatty: location, contacts, sensors, all reporting home by default. There are two sane answers: an iPhone configured deliberately, or a de-Googled Android. Which one is right is a pure threat-model question.

    before you pick Be honest about what you'll live with. iOS asks nothing of you and covers the vast majority of threat models out of the box. Flashing a custom Android OS is a weekend project with real stakes: banking apps may object, there's no support line, and the friction is daily. The technically superior option you abandon in month two protects less than the good-enough one you keep.

    what actually matters

    update speed

    Security patches matter more than any privacy feature. Days-behind-upstream is the standard to hold; Apple and GrapheneOS both meet it.

    hardware security

    Secure Enclave, Titan chips, verified boot: cryptographic key isolation and tamper-proof boot are hardware properties, not settings.

    ecosystem exposure

    It's about who the OS reports to by default, and whether you can turn it off. None at all, sandboxed and optional, or "trust Apple entirely" are all different answers.

    app compatibility

    It's the friction people underestimate until they live with it daily. Banking apps, tap-to-pay, and that one work app decide more setups than security specs do.

    recommendations

    iOS

    the default pick
    🍎 apple onlyclosed sourcefree

    iOS is the software on iPhones, and it's one of the easiest ways to get strong privacy and security without doing much setup. Apple keeps your encryption keys in a protected part of the chip called the Secure Enclave, away from the rest of the phone. It also controls what apps can access more tightly than most phone makers do. Apple Pay uses a stand-in number (a token) instead of your real card number. For most people, including those with serious security needs, it's a sensible pick because their apps keep working.

    Newer iPhones add extra protection. The iPhone 17 and iPhone Air have Memory Integrity Enforcement, which blocks a common kind of attack that abuses software bugs. Lockdown Mode is an optional setting for people who may be targeted because of who they are or what they do. Since June 2026, Apple has also been running some Apple Intelligence (its AI features) requests on Google Cloud servers, as a preview. Apple says the same privacy rules apply.

    good
    • Hardware-level key isolation (Secure Enclave) on every device
    • Strictly enforced sandbox and permission prompts
    • Apple Pay tokenization beats standard card transactions
    • Fast, long-lived updates with zero effort from you
    • Lockdown Mode: optional extreme protection against spyware
    mind the
    • Closed source; the trust model is "Apple, entirely": a FAANG dependency
    • No user-controlled OS attestation
    • iCloud backups (including iMessages) are not end-to-end encrypted until you turn on Advanced Data Protection
    • Advanced Data Protection is unavailable in the UK
    os free · new hardware from ~€850 apple.com/privacy →

    GrapheneOS

    the maximum-security pick
    open sourceverified bootsandboxed playfree

    GrapheneOS is a security-focused version of Android that runs on Google Pixel phones. It's the technically superior choice if you worry about a government-level attacker or a seized phone, or you want proof that the system hasn't been tampered with. It makes common hacking tricks harder, keeps apps walled off from each other, and gets security fixes within days. Google's apps are optional, and if you add them they run like any other app, with no special access.

    The catch is that some apps don't work well on it, and many people don't realize how much that matters until they live with it daily. If you can accept that, nothing else comes close. GrapheneOS recommends Pixel 8 or newer, which get seven years of updates.

    It also has a duress PIN, a second PIN that wipes the phone when someone enters it. Reports in July 2026 said a US activist faces a federal charge after a duress PIN wiped a phone during a 2025 airport search. The activist pleaded not guilty, and GrapheneOS says the feature is legal.

    good
    • Maximum verifiable security and privacy in consumer mobile
    • Sandboxed Google Play: compatibility without the privileges
    • Verified boot, user-controlled attestation, full OTA updates
    • Per-app network/sensor kill switches, storage scopes, duress PIN
    mind the
    • Pixel hardware only; Motorola flagships planned for 2027, none shipped
    • Google's support promise for Pixel 6 and 6 Pro ended October 2026
    • Some banking apps refuse non-stock OSes; no Google Wallet tap-to-pay
    • The friction is daily, not just at setup; be honest about your tolerance
    free · pixel from ~€350 used/refurb grapheneos.org →

    CalyxOS

    the middle-ground pick
    open sourcenonprofitfree

    CalyxOS is a version of Android with Google's tracking removed, built to be easy to live with. It works well right after you install it, with little tinkering. It includes microG, a free stand-in for Google's background services. It's optional, and it lets notifications and location work in most apps without your phone talking to Google directly. CalyxOS is less locked down than GrapheneOS but easier to start with: a halfway house between a regular Android phone and the deep end.

    The project went quiet for most of a year. It paused in summer 2025 after the Calyx Institute's founder and CalyxOS's lead developer left, and the team decided to rebuild the keys it uses to sign its software. Official releases returned on 1 July 2026 (Android 16), and Android 17 began rolling out on 1 October. If you're on an older build, you need to reinstall.

    good
    • microG covers most app expectations with far less Google exposure
    • Supports Fairphone, Motorola, and SHIFT phones, not just Pixels
    • Thoughtful defaults: Datura firewall per app, Tor integration
    mind the
    • No official releases for most of a year: paused 2025, back July 2026
    • May 2026 build left out CalyxVPN and the Chromium adblocker
    • microG gives weaker isolation than sandboxed Play
    • Monthly patches on Pixels; other phones a month or two behind
    • Smaller team; fewer devices than Lineage
    free calyxos.org →

    LineageOS

    the old-hardware pick
    open sourcefree

    LineageOS is a free, community-made version of Android with no Google apps. It keeps abandoned phones alive: a seven-year-old phone can run a recent version of Android, stay private, and stay out of the landfill. LineageOS 24, based on Android 17, launched in October 2026 for a growing list of phones.

    The oldest phones have limits. Those built on the Linux 4.4 and 4.9 kernels (the core of the system) are now capped at LineageOS 22.2, which is based on Android 15. Only LineageOS 21 and newer carry a full monthly security patch label. Most phones also give up verified boot, a startup check that the system hasn't been altered. That makes LineageOS a good way to save old hardware and a weaker choice if security is your top concern.

    good
    • Enormous device support, your drawer phone probably qualifies
    • Ships Google-free by default; you choose what to add
    • Ten years old this December, with CyanogenMod heritage
    • Unaffected by Google's developer verification (no Google apps)
    mind the
    • Verified boot usually lost; bootloader stays unlocked on most devices
    • Patch speed varies per device maintainer
    • Oldest-kernel phones are capped at Android 15 (LineageOS 22.2)
    • Quality differs wildly across the device list, research yours first
    free lineageos.org →

    worth knowing

    On iOS, the checklist is short but real. Turn on Advanced Data Protection (Settings → your name → iCloud → Advanced Data Protection) so backups are actually end-to-end encrypted, audit app permissions quarterly, and turn off personalized ads (Settings → Privacy & Security → Apple Advertising). That's most of the gap closed.

    On de-Googled Android, get apps from open stores first. Use F-Droid (or the nicer Droid-ify client) for open-source apps, and Aurora Store for anonymous Play access. On GrapheneOS, sandboxed Play is the cleanest route for the stubborn few.

    The OS doesn't fix the apps. Instagram on GrapheneOS still tells Meta everything you do in Instagram, and the same goes for iOS. Pair the OS choice with an app diet and the DNS filtering from the network page.

    Mind the SIM. Your carrier logs tower locations regardless of OS. For most people that's accepted; for high-risk models it's the reason burner SIMs and Wi-Fi-only devices exist.
