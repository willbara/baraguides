    Social Media

    last updated 2026-11-04 · 5 recommendations + nostr clients · what changed

    Mainstream social media (Twitter/X, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok) runs on the same trade: you get a free feed, the platform gets your attention, and an algorithm decides what you see in order to maximize it. The alternatives here are decentralized and non-corporate: you own your account, nobody is optimizing your feed for engagement, and no single company can sell access to your attention.

    before you pick Federation means no single company can flip a switch and delete the whole network, but it also means moderation quality varies by server. You're not just picking an app. You're picking a community run by whoever operates that instance. Read their rules before you commit your account to one.

    what actually matters

    federation / activitypub

    No single company owns the network. Servers run by different people and organizations talk to each other over a shared protocol.

    no algorithmic engagement feed

    You get chronological or user-controlled timelines, not a black-box ranking system tuned to maximize time-on-app.

    data portability

    Can you take your followers and posts with you if you move servers? Real portability means no instance can hold your social graph hostage.

    instance moderation quality

    Federation pushes moderation down to individual server admins. A good network of well-run instances beats one big company's policy team, but only if you pick a well-run instance.

    plain-english key

    ActivityPub

    The shared language that lets different servers and apps talk to each other. It is why a Mastodon account can follow a PeerTube channel.

    Fediverse

    The whole network of servers and apps that speak ActivityPub. Mastodon, Lemmy, PeerTube, and Pixelfed are all part of it.

    instance

    One server running the software, run by a person or group with its own rules. Your account lives on an instance.

    relay and client (Nostr)

    A relay is a simple server that stores posts and passes them along. A client is the app you read and post with. You can mix any client with any relay.

    keypair (Nostr)

    Your private key proves you are you, and your public key is the name others see. Whoever holds the private key controls the account, so losing it means losing the account.

    Lightning and zaps

    Lightning is a faster way to send small Bitcoin payments. A zap is a small tip sent that way. In a custodial wallet a company holds the money for you. In a non-custodial wallet you hold it.

    recommendations

    Mastodon

    the default pick
    federatedno algorithmic feedopen sourcefree

    Mastodon is the closest thing to a Twitter/X replacement that no single company owns. Anyone can run the software. The servers (instances) that run it talk to each other over ActivityPub. Following someone on a different server works the same as following someone on your own. Timelines are chronological, not ranked by an algorithm. You can also move your account, followers included, to another instance if yours goes downhill.

    Which instance you pick matters more here than on any centralized platform. It sets your moderation experience, your default visibility, and who has admin access to your account. Founder Eugen Rochko stepped down as CEO in November 2025 and now advises, and Felix Hlatky is the executive director. Mastodon said then that a US nonprofit holds the trademark for now, with a Belgian nonprofit planned to take it over.

    good
    • Federated: no single company can shut down the whole network
    • Chronological timeline, no engagement-optimized algorithm
    • Account migration tools let you move instances without losing followers
    • Funded by donations, with no ads
    • Quote posts since November 2025, and authors control who can quote them
    mind the
    • Moderation quality and culture vary a lot between instances
    • Weaker discovery than algorithm-driven platforms
    • Active users have fallen to under 1M
    • Picking an instance is an extra decision newcomers don't expect
    free, instance-dependent joinmastodon.org →

    Lemmy

    the reddit-alternative pick
    federatedno algorithmic feedopen sourceself-hostablefree

    Lemmy plays the role Reddit plays, with link sharing and threaded discussion organized into communities. It runs across independently run instances instead of belonging to one company. Communities (Lemmy's subforums) work across servers, so joining one on someone else's instance works the same as joining a local one. There's no ad-driven feed ranking. You sort by votes, new, or active, same as on the platform it replaces.

    The stable release is still the 0.19 line (0.19.21, October 2026), and a major 1.0 version has been in beta since May 2026. Lemmy had many security advisories in 2026, and two were rated high. One of them, fixed in 0.19.21, let a remote server delete any account on an instance. Check that your instance keeps up with updates.

    good
    • Federated communities: no single company controls the forums
    • Sorted by votes or time, not an algorithm
    • Open source and self-hostable
    • Security flaws get public advisories and fixes on GitHub
    mind the
    • Far smaller than Reddit, with many thin communities
    • Moderation and server-blocking decisions vary widely by instance
    • Some niche communities simply don't exist yet
    • Many security fixes in 2026, including two rated high
    free, instance-dependent join-lemmy.org →

    PeerTube

    the youtube-alternative pick
    federatedno algorithmic feedopen sourceself-hostablefree

    PeerTube is federated video hosting. Any instance can host and publish videos. Instances connect to each other, so you can browse and subscribe to channels across servers from one account. No recommendation algorithm chases watch time, so you see what the creators you subscribe to, and your instance, actually posted. Some instances also let viewers' browsers share video pieces with each other (peer-to-peer). That spreads the bandwidth cost instead of leaving it all on one server.

    PeerTube shipped several critical security fixes in 2026, the latest in version 8.3.2 on October 26. That release closed a flaw in an image library that a crafted file could use to run code on the server. The PeerTube team says security releases are coming faster partly because of AI-assisted vulnerability scans. If you use an instance, check that its admin keeps it updated.

    good
    • Federated: no single company removes or demonetizes videos network-wide
    • No engagement-optimized recommendation algorithm
    • Open source and self-hostable
    • Optional peer-to-peer streaming spreads bandwidth costs
    • Mobile app: background playback, casting, and phone uploads
    mind the
    • Catalog and creator base are tiny next to YouTube
    • Video quality and availability depend on the instance's resources
    • Discovery across instances is clunkier than a unified platform
    • Phone live streaming needs a separate Android-only app
    • Several critical security fixes in 2026
    free, instance-dependent joinpeertube.org →

    Pixelfed

    the instagram-alternative pick
    federatedno algorithmic feedopen sourcefree

    Pixelfed is photo-first sharing built on the same federated model as Mastodon. Thanks to ActivityPub, you can follow a Pixelfed account from Mastodon, and the other way around, for basic interactions. The feed is chronological by default instead of an algorithm deciding which posts you're "likely to engage with." No ad business model pushes the design toward maximizing your scroll time.

    Daniel Supernault created Pixelfed and still leads it. A 2025 Kickstarter raised CA$138,742, nearly three times its goal, to build a team and register a nonprofit. Version 0.12.5 (March 2025) fixed a flaw in locked accounts. Any Pixelfed user could follow one without approval and see its followers-only posts. Hachyderm, a large Mastodon server, cut ties with 63 Pixelfed servers that had not yet updated.

    good
    • Federated: works with Mastodon and the wider Fediverse
    • Chronological timeline, no engagement-ranking algorithm
    • No ad-driven business model shaping the feed
    • Founder turned down venture capital offers in January 2025 to stay independent
    mind the
    • Small team: one founder leads development
    • Smaller network: fewer accounts to follow than Instagram
    • Official apps only launched in January 2025
    • Instance choice affects storage limits and moderation, same as Mastodon
    • Followers-only posts were exposed by a flaw fixed in March 2025
    free, instance-dependent pixelfed.org →

    Nostr

    the key-based pick
    decentralizedno algorithmic feedopen sourcefree

    Nostr is a social protocol where your identity is a cryptographic key you generate yourself. You publish signed posts to relays, plain servers that store and pass along posts without talking to each other. That means there are no instances and nothing to migrate between. It's a stronger kind of portability than the Fediverse offers, since your identity and followers live in the key instead of on a server. The risk grows to match, because a lost or leaked key can't be fixed. Anyone can build an app for the protocol, so your experience depends on the client you pick, such as the two covered below.

    good
    • You hold your own keypair: no server can suspend you
    • Any client works with any relay, and switching either costs you nothing
    • No ads and no ranking algorithm at the protocol level
    • Cheap, interchangeable relays are hard to silence
    • Cure53 audited NIP-44, the current message-encryption standard, in 2023
    mind the
    • Lose your private key and it's gone: no reset, no key-rotation standard
    • Relays see your IP address unless you use Tor or a VPN
    • Deleted posts may survive on relays that ignore the request
    • Free, unlimited identities make spam and impersonation hard to stop
    • Low tens of thousands of daily users, with a heavy Bitcoin culture
    free · open protocol nostr.com →

    nostr clients

    Primal

    the cross-platform client
    decentralizedall platformsopen sourcefree tier

    Primal is a Nostr client built by Miljan Braticevic's venture-backed company, which launched it with a $1M seed round in 2023. It runs on the web, iPhone, and Android from a single account. Primal runs its own indexing server (a server that catalogs posts), which powers its search and also makes it a soft point of centralization. It has a built-in Bitcoin Lightning wallet. In March 2026 Primal replaced the old wallet, where Primal held your money, with one it calls non-custodial, where you hold it.

    good
    • Best search and discovery on Nostr
    • Web, iOS, and Android from a single account
    • The whole stack is open source, caching server included
    • Much gentler onboarding than the rest of the ecosystem
    mind the
    • Its indexing server is a soft point of centralization
    • Fewer relay controls than clients built for power users
    • Built-in Lightning wallet is hard to ignore
    • Venture-backed: how it will make money is still open
    free · premium tier available primal.net →

    Damus

    the relay-transparent client
    decentralizedall platformsopen sourcefree tier

    Damus is a Nostr client maintained by Will Casarin, who is also one of Nostr's core protocol developers. OpenSats grants have funded it since 2023. Damus began on iPhone and was the first Nostr app in Apple's App Store, in February 2023. A Chinese regulator had it removed from China's App Store days later. Its desktop app, Notedeck, is still a beta, and its latest release is from April 2026. Its spam filtering relies on the accounts you follow, so a new account sees more spam.

    good
    • Maintained by a core protocol developer and funded by grants, not investors
    • Runs on iOS, Android, and desktop
    • Connects only to relays you choose
    • Shows which relays carried a post
    • Web-of-trust filtering makes spam manageable without central moderation
    • Banned from China's App Store in 2023
    mind the
    • Android and desktop apps are newer and less polished
    • Older versions had flaws reported in a 2025 academic paper
    • Some features sit behind the optional Purple subscription
    • New accounts see more spam
    free · optional subscription damus.io →

    "federation" here means independently run servers interoperating over a shared protocol, not one company's infrastructure.

    worth knowing

    These are all part of the Fediverse. Mastodon, Lemmy, PeerTube, and Pixelfed all speak ActivityPub to varying degrees, which means they interoperate: you can often follow a PeerTube channel or a Pixelfed account from a Mastodon account, for instance. It's a network of networks, not one app.

    Picking an instance matters as much as picking the app. Read the instance's rules and moderation history before you commit your account to it. A small, well-moderated instance is usually a better experience than a huge, under-moderated one.

    Account migration tools exist. Mastodon in particular lets you move your account (including your follower list) to a different instance if the one you're on changes hands, shuts down, or just isn't a good fit anymore. That portability is the whole point of federation.
