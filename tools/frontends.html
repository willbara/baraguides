    Privacy Frontends

    last updated 2026-11-04 · 7 tools · what changed

    You don't need an account, an app, or an ad profile to watch a YouTube video or read a Reddit thread. Frontends are alternative interfaces to the big platforms: same content, different window, none of the tracking scripts.

    before you pick Frontends live in a cat-and-mouse game with the platforms they read from. Google has been blocking Invidious instances at the IP level since 2024. In October 2026, Redlib users began reporting blocked comment pages, and on 29 October Reddit said it is moving to a logged-in-only experience, with tests outside the US first. X sent Nitter cease-and-desist letters in August and September 2026. Its main instances went offline, and the maintainer is seeking legal help. Treat any single public instance as disposable and keep fallbacks.

    what actually matters

    where it runs

    A public web instance sees your traffic; a local app like FreeTube or NewPipe keeps everything on your device. When you have the choice, local wins.

    instance trust

    Whoever hosts an instance can log what you read and watch. You're swapping Google's logging for a volunteer's, so pick maintained instances or self-host.

    breakage risk

    Frontends scrape or use unofficial APIs. When the platform changes something, the frontend breaks until the maintainers catch up.

    feature parity

    Subscriptions, playlists, and history can live locally with no account. Posting, voting, and comments mostly can't.

    plain-english key

    instance

    One public copy of a frontend, run by a volunteer on their own server. Whoever runs it can see the requests that pass through it.

    self-hosting

    Running the frontend yourself, on a home computer or a rented server, so the only person who sees your traffic is you.

    extractor

    The code that pulls video details and streams out of YouTube. Every YouTube frontend here has one, and it breaks when YouTube changes something.

    SABR

    YouTube's newer way of sending video. Tools built for the older way stopped working until their makers added support for it in 2026.

    Manifest V2 and V3

    Two versions of the rulebook for browser extensions. Chrome and most browsers built on it are removing the older V2, which is why older extensions stop working there.

    the tools

    LibRedirect

    start here
    browser extensionopen sourcefree

    LibRedirect is a browser extension that automatically rewrites links to the big platforms so they open in a privacy frontend instead. You click a YouTube link and land on Invidious; you click a Reddit thread and land on Redlib. It's the glue that makes the rest of this page usable day to day.

    The maintainers list Firefox, Brave, and Helium (another Chromium-based browser) as supported. Chrome and most other Chromium browsers can't run it, because it still uses Manifest V2, the older extension format. The two maintainers say a move to V3 is planned, but they lack the time right now, so the project is in maintenance mode. Brave changed how it treats older extensions in 2026, so check that it still runs there.

    good
    • Covers far more than video and Reddit: Wikipedia, TikTok, Medium, translation, maps, search
    • Per-service instance lists update automatically from a dedicated repo
    • You can set your own instances and fallbacks per service
    mind the
    • Doesn't work on Chrome or most Chromium browsers
    • Two-person maintenance-mode team, with no date for a Chrome rewrite
    • Only as good as the instances it points at: keep two or three per service
    free · open source libredirect.manerakai.com →

    Redlib

    the reddit pick
    web-basedself-hostableopen sourcefree

    Redlib is a lightweight Reddit frontend and the actively maintained successor to Libreddit. You browse subreddits and threads with no account, no ads, and no infinite-scroll tricks. You can't post or vote through it; it's for reading.

    Redlib has run into trouble. Since 20 October 2026, users have reported Reddit rejecting its requests for comment pages. Reports since then are mixed: some instances and forks work, and others don't. On 29 October, Reddit's shareholder letter said it is shifting to a logged-in-only experience, with tests outside the US first. Whether the two are linked is unconfirmed. Redlib's latest release came on 22 October, its first since March 2025.

    good
    • Server-rendered pages with zero client-side JavaScript
    • Poses as Reddit's official Android app, refreshing its access token daily
    • Latest release adds anti-blocking tweaks and proxy support
    • Community instance list updates automatically
    mind the
    • Read-only by design: no posting, voting, or commenting
    • Reports of blocked comment pages since 20 October 2026
    • Login-gated communities can't be reached by any frontend
    • Busy public instances get rate-limited by Reddit
    free · open source github.com/redlib-org →

    Invidious

    the full-interface pick
    web-basedself-hostableopen sourcefree

    Invidious is a web frontend for YouTube that plays videos without ads or tracking scripts. Pick it when you want the whole experience in a browser; pick Piped when you just want playback that degrades gracefully.

    YouTube's blocking has thinned the public list. The official site lists only four regular web instances in November 2026. Playback also relies on invidious-companion, a separate helper program that fetches the video streams, so running your own means running two pieces.

    good
    • The fullest interface here: comments, channel pages, playlists
    • Local accounts keep subscriptions without touching Google
    • RSS feeds per channel
    • Actively developed: new releases in October and November 2026
    mind the
    • Google has blocked it at the IP level since 2024: captchas, throttling, blacklists
    • Most public instances lock registration just to control the load
    • A self-hosted personal instance works better than any public one
    • Playback needs the separate invidious-companion helper
    free · open source invidious.io →

    Piped

    the self-host pick
    web-basedself-hostableopen sourcefree

    Piped is a YouTube frontend split into a frontend, a backend, and a proxy layer, so the video stream reaches you through the instance instead of straight from Google's servers. Plan to self-host it, because public instances are unreliable. On 4 November 2026, seven of the 15 instances on Piped's official list no longer existed at their listed addresses, and only two returned video data in a test.

    good
    • The proxy layer degrades more gracefully than Invidious when Google blocks a request
    • Local subscriptions and playlists, importable from a Google takeout
    • Your traffic never touches Google's servers directly
    mind the
    • Seven of 15 listed public instances no longer exist
    • LibreTube dropped public Piped instances in April 2026
    • Self-hosting is effectively the only reliable way to run it
    free · open source github.com/TeamPiped →

    FreeTube

    the desktop pick
    desktop applocal-firstopen sourcefree

    FreeTube is a desktop app for YouTube on Windows, macOS, and Linux. Subscriptions, history, and playlists live in local files on your machine instead of on anyone's server. If you watch YouTube at a desk, this is the comfortable option.

    Since July 2026 the team has shipped several fixes to keep playback working after YouTube moved to SABR, its newer way of sending video. YouTube's bot checks can also show a CAPTCHA (a "prove you're human" puzzle) instead of the video.

    good
    • Everything stays in local files; there's no server side to trust
    • Built-in extractor by default, with Invidious as an optional primary or fallback
    • Official installers for all three desktop platforms
    • LibRedirect can route YouTube links straight into it
    mind the
    • Labeled Beta by the project itself
    • Desktop only; pair it with NewPipe or LibreTube on the phone
    • Live streams play again since November 2026, but you can't seek
    • Version 0.26 dropped macOS 12 support
    free · open source freetubeapp.io →

    NewPipe

    the android pick
    android onlyf-droidopen sourcefree

    NewPipe is the long-standing Android frontend for YouTube. It streams without ads and keeps subscriptions on the device, with no Google account involved. You install it from F-Droid or its own repository, not the Play Store.

    Three 2026 changes matter. YouTube's move to SABR (its newer way of sending video) cut available resolutions, and the June fix works for everything except videos made for kids. Version 0.29 in July dropped Android 5, so you now need Android 6 or newer. And Google's developer verification, which makes app developers register with Google, began on 30 September 2026. Its first phase covers installs from Google Play and a few phone-maker stores in four countries. Other sources follow in 2027. NewPipe's team opposes the program and says it won't enroll.

    good
    • Background play and picture-in-picture without Premium
    • Downloads video and audio straight to the device
    • Also reads SoundCloud, PeerTube, Bandcamp, and media.ccc.de
    mind the
    • Seeking maintainers for its SoundCloud and PeerTube support
    • Not on the Play Store; updates come from F-Droid or its own repo
    • Breaks when YouTube changes things, until a fix ships
    • Team won't join Google's developer verification
    free · open source newpipe.net →

    LibreTube

    the android alternative
    android onlyf-droidopen sourcefree

    LibreTube is a newer Android client for YouTube with a more modern Material design than NewPipe. It started life on the Piped backend. Since version 31 in April 2026, it talks to YouTube directly through the NewPipe Extractor library (code that pulls video data out of YouTube). That means it no longer depends on public instances.

    Version 32 in August 2026 added support for SABR, YouTube's newer way of sending video, to fix playback failures. It installs from F-Droid, IzzyOnDroid, or GitHub.

    good
    • Talks to YouTube directly, with no Piped instance needed
    • Same no-account subscription model, wrapped in a current Material design
    • Active: stable releases in April, May, and August 2026
    • Can still point at a self-hosted Piped instance if you run one
    mind the
    • Dropped public Piped instance support in April 2026
    • Shorter track record than NewPipe
    • Shares NewPipe's extractor library, and its breakages
    free · open source libretube.dev →

    worth knowing

    Local beats hosted. If a native app exists for your platform, prefer it over a public web instance: your watch history never leaves the device and there's no third party to trust.

    Never log in through a frontend. Frontends are for reading. If a page asks for your platform credentials, you're either on the wrong site or about to hand your password to a stranger.

    Use the projects' own instance lists. No trustworthy cross-tool instance tracker exists, so lean on each project's own list (LibRedirect and Redlib update theirs automatically). Add two or three instances per service to LibRedirect so a dead one is an annoyance instead of a dead end.

    You still have to trust someone. A public instance host sees your IP and your requests even when your browser runs no tracking scripts, and the platform can see the instance in turn: a Redlib instance inherits Reddit's rate limits on its own IP, so heavy shared use is visible to Reddit. Pair instances with a VPN if that bothers you, or stick to the local apps.

    Android installs may change in 2027. NewPipe and LibreTube both come from outside the Play Store. Google's developer verification asks app developers to register. Since 30 September 2026 it applies to installs from Google Play and six phone-maker stores in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore, and Thailand. Google says direct downloads and other stores are covered when it goes global in 2027. Apps from unregistered developers will then need a one-time "advanced flow" to install: developer mode, a one-day wait, and a warning screen. Google says installing from a computer with ADB (Android's developer tool) is unchanged. F-Droid and NewPipe have both publicly opposed the program.
