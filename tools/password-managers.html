    Password Managers

    last updated 2026-11-04 · 4 recommendations + 2 mainstream managers · what changed

    If you do exactly one thing from this entire site, do this. A password manager gives every account a strong, unique password you never have to remember, so one leaked site can't unlock the rest of your life.

    before you pick Any manager on this page beats reusing passwords, including the one built into your browser. Don't agonize over the choice; the win is using one at all. Pick, set a long memorable master passphrase, and turn on 2FA for the vault itself.

    what actually matters

    audits & track record

    You're trusting this thing with everything. Open code is one route to that trust; relentless third-party audits and a clean history are another. Insist on at least one.

    zero-knowledge encryption

    The vault must be encrypted with your master password before it leaves your device. The provider should never be able to read it.

    company trajectory

    A vault is a decade-long relationship. Leadership changes, quiet edits to public promises, and buyouts by investment firms (private equity) matter as much as the cryptography.

    export & escape

    You should be able to export your vault to a standard format anytime. Lock-in on a password manager is lock-in on everything.

    plain-english key

    open source

    The code is public, so anyone can read it for flaws. "Source-available" means you can read it but can't freely reuse or change it.

    passkey

    A login that replaces the password with a key kept on your device, unlocked by your fingerprint, face, or PIN.

    TOTP (2FA codes)

    The six-digit codes an authenticator app makes every 30 seconds. They act as a second login step, called two-factor authentication (2FA).

    clickjacking

    A malicious page hides your manager's autofill menu behind something you click, which can send your saved details to the attacker.

    supply-chain attack

    Attackers tamper with software on its way to users, such as a poisoned download, instead of breaking into the company's own servers.

    hijacked-server test

    Researchers check what an attacker who fully controls a company's servers could do to your vault. Zero-knowledge designs are meant to hold up here.

    recommendations

    Bitwarden

    the default pick
    🇺🇸 usaopen sourceauditedself-hostablefree tier

    Bitwarden is technically strong. Its code is public on GitHub, outside firms audit it every year, and you can host it yourself. In 2026 a wave of leadership changes brought a new CEO from private equity (investment-firm buyouts), which raised fears of a sale. Bitwarden says it isn't seeking a buyer, and no sale has followed.

    On 9 October 2026 Bitwarden said its app-store apps will become commercially licensed builds. The GPLv3 open-source version stays on GitHub, and Bitwarden says self-hosting and the free plan are unaffected. Some future features may be commercial-only, and its own FAQ calls that license "source available". A February 2026 ETH Zurich study of hijacked servers found weaknesses that Bitwarden rates medium or low and is mostly fixing.

    good
    • The long-time gold standard
    • Code public on GitHub, with annual third-party audits
    • Usable free tier: unlimited entries and devices
    • Self-hosting keeps you independent of the company
    mind the
    • Unannounced leadership changes and quietly edited public commitments
    • App-store builds now commercially licensed (October 2026)
    • Premium price nearly doubled in January 2026, first rise in a decade
    • April 2026 attack briefly compromised its command-line tool (no vault data affected)
    free · premium ~$19.80/yr bitwarden.com →

    1Password

    the premium pick
    🇨🇦 canadaclosed sourceauditedpaid

    1Password is the most polished manager here, with the smoothest apps and autofill, built on a hardened and thoroughly audited design. Its "Secret Key" is a long random code kept only on your own devices. A stolen master password alone can't unlock your vault. The trade-off is real. Closed source and subscription-only is an ideological compromise, but an acceptable one given the company's record and what's happening at Bitwarden above.

    One catch is clickjacking. In 2025 a researcher showed that several popular browser-extension managers could be tricked into leaking saved logins. 1Password added opt-in confirmation prompts instead of closing the hole. The researcher's tracker still listed it as exploitable in January 2026. The February 2026 ETH Zurich hijacked-server study also found weaknesses in 1Password. It calls them known limitations, already described in its security white paper.

    good
    • Best-in-class apps, autofill, and onboarding
    • Secret Key adds real protection against server-side breaches
    • Regular public audit reports and a strong security team
    • Travel Mode hides chosen vaults at border crossings
    mind the
    • Closed source: outsiders can't read the code
    • 2025 clickjacking flaw in its browser extension only partly fixed
    • March 2026 price rise: about 33% Individual, 20% Families
    from ~$48/yr billed annually · families ~$72/yr billed annually 1password.com →

    KeePassXC

    the local-only pick
    local-firstopen sourceno accounttotpfree

    KeePassXC has no cloud, no account, and no company. Your vault is a single encrypted file on your own disk. Nothing leaves your machine unless you move it. Even if you run 1Password or Bitwarden day to day, keep a periodic KeePassXC export as a local encrypted backup. It keeps your credentials independent of any single vendor's future, which is exactly the risk the entries above are wrestling with.

    France's national cybersecurity agency (ANSSI) certified version 2.7.9 on Windows 10 in November 2025, and newer versions are outside that certificate. Version 2.8.0 (October 2026) fixed five low-to-moderate vulnerabilities, none in the core encryption, so update.

    good
    • Zero trust in any third party: the file never leaves you
    • Open KDBX file format that many apps can read
    • Built-in TOTP, passkey support, browser integration
    • Certified by France's cybersecurity agency (ANSSI), November 2025
    • No business model to rot, no boardroom to watch
    mind the
    • You handle sync and backups yourself
    • A lost file or master password means a lost vault
    • No official mobile app (KeePassDX and others fill in)
    • Less convenient for sharing credentials with family
    free, donation-funded keepassxc.org →

    Proton Pass

    the ecosystem pick
    🇨🇭 switzerlandpartially open sourceauditedaliasingfree tier

    Proton Pass is part of the Proton suite, with open-source clients and end-to-end encryption across the board. The standout feature is built-in hide-my-email aliasing. Every login can get its own forwarding address, generated next to its password, with no separate alias service required. If you're already on Proton Mail, Drive, or VPN, Pass slots into the same account and billing with zero extra setup.

    Proton has also faced fresh criticism. In October 2026, 404 Media reported that CEO Andy Yen called a request to security-test new code "loser thinking". Proton later admitted it had been too blunt. The Proton Mail card has more.

    good
    • Open-source apps across every platform
    • Audited by Cure53 (2023) and Recurity Labs (2026, "well above par")
    • Built-in email aliases next to each password
    • Fits neatly with the rest of Proton
    • Usable free tier, and cheap beside a Proton plan
    mind the
    • Newer product, so a shorter audit history
    • Most appealing if you already use Proton
    • Free plan includes only 10 email aliases
    free tier · paid from ~$2.99/mo billed annually proton.me/pass →

    Dashlane

    the mainstream one, not a pick
    🇺🇸 usazero-knowledgeclosed sourcepaid

    Dashlane is a subscription password manager and digital wallet with autofill, dark web monitoring, passkey support, and a bundled VPN (a rebranded Hotspot Shield). It's on this page the way Telegram is on the messengers page: popular enough that you should know exactly where it stands.

    Its record cuts both ways. In May 2026 an attacker brute-forced the two-factor checks for adding a new device and copied the encrypted vaults of fewer than 20 accounts. The vaults stayed locked, but stolen copies can still be attacked offline. It was the company's first confirmed customer-facing incident. The February 2026 ETH Zurich hijacked-server test also covered Dashlane, which fixed one weakness and acknowledged two it hasn't fixed. It doesn't publish a named-firm audit like Bitwarden and 1Password do. Your contract sits with Dashlane USA in New York or Dashlane SAS in Paris, depending on where you're billed.

    good
    • Stolen vaults stayed encrypted in the 2026 attack
    • Client app source on GitHub (non-commercial license, closed server)
    • Friends & Family covers 10 people, versus 5 or 6 elsewhere
    mind the
    • No published audit of the encryption by a named firm
    • May 2026: attacker copied encrypted vaults from under 20 accounts
    • No free plan since September 2025
    • Annual billing only
    • Can't buy the password manager without the VPN bundle
    premium ~$4.99/mo billed annually · friends & family ~$7.49/mo billed annually dashlane.com →

    NordPass

    the mainstream Nord-family pick
    closed sourcezero-knowledgefree tier

    NordPass is the password manager from Nord Security, the company behind NordVPN. It uses modern encryption (XChaCha20) and a deliberately slow password-scrambling step (Argon2id) that makes guessing your master password expensive, inside a zero-knowledge design.

    It's closed source, so unlike Bitwarden or Proton Pass you can't read the code and have to trust the company's audits and its word. Its independent audits on public record are Cure53's, from 2020 and 2021. NordPass itself has no publicly known breach. The 2018 breach that hit NordVPN involved a different product on separate infrastructure.

    good
    • Modern encryption: XChaCha20 with Argon2id
    • No publicly known breach of NordPass itself
    • Listed as fixed for the 2025 clickjacking flaw
    • Inexpensive, with a genuine free tier
    mind the
    • Closed source: you trust the audits and the company's word
    • Latest independent audits on record date from 2020 and 2021
    • Heavily promotional pricing, with no published renewal rate
    • Jurisdiction is layered: Lithuanian entity, Panama-associated brand, offshore ownership beyond that
    prices change often · check nordpass.com nordpass.com →

    worth knowing

    The master passphrase is the whole game. Make it long and memorable: four or five random words beats P@ssw0rd2026! by miles. Write it down and store the paper somewhere physically safe until it sticks.

    Protect the vault with 2FA. A hardware key or TOTP app on the manager itself means a phished master password still isn't enough. See 2FA & Hardware Keys.

    Keep a vendor-proof backup. Export your vault to an encrypted KeePassXC database every few months and store it locally. Companies get acquired, policies change, accounts get locked; a local copy turns all of those from emergencies into inconveniences.

    Save the recovery kit. Most managers generate recovery codes or an emergency sheet at signup. Print it. Locking yourself out is the most common way people lose a vault.
