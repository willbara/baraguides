    ~/tools/data-removal

    Personal Data Removers

    last updated 2026-11-04 · 3 recommendations · what changed

    Data broker sites pull together and resell your name, address, phone number, relatives, and property records to anyone who pays. Removal services automate the opt-out requests across hundreds of these brokers so you're not filing them by hand one at a time.

    before you pick Manage expectations: these services reduce exposure but can't guarantee permanent removal. Brokers re-scrape public records (copy them again from public sources), so a name removed today can resurface within months. That's why this category is a subscription: the removal work has to be repeated.

    what actually matters

    breadth of broker coverage

    The internet has hundreds of data brokers, and new ones appear constantly. More coverage means fewer gaps for old data to hide in.

    automation vs. manual opt-outs

    Some brokers accept automated requests; others require a human to fill out a form or mail a letter. A service that does both covers more ground.

    recurring re-removal & monitoring

    Removal isn't permanent. The service needs to keep checking and re-filing as brokers re-list you, not just run once at signup.

    recommendations

    EasyOptOuts

    the recommended pick
    paid

    EasyOptOuts is this page's default recommendation, mostly on price. For a flat $19.99 a year, it re-runs your opt-outs every four months, far below the other two entries. It has no flashy upsells, just the opt-out work. Its site says two independent people run it, with no outside funding.

    Independent testers rate it well. In Consumer Reports' August 2024 test of seven services, it was one of the two best, removing 65 percent of profiles after four months. Privacy Guides calls it the best value of any service it has tested. Its site says it covers "200+" high-visibility data brokers and people-search sites, some indirectly. That is fewer than Incogni's 420+ or DeleteMe's 969-site list (which spans all its plans), which is the trade-off for the price. It only works for US residents.

    Incogni

    the broad-coverage pick
    paid

    Incogni sends automated opt-out requests to 420+ data brokers on every plan, one of the broadest lists in the category. Surfshark built it in-house. Surfshark merged with Nord Security in 2022 under a joint holding company called Cyberspace, so Incogni now sits alongside NordVPN in the Nord group. If you'd rather not concentrate your privacy tools under one company, that's worth knowing.

    In August 2025, Deloitte (an accounting firm) issued a limited-assurance report (a lighter check than a full audit) on Incogni's process, as of 18 July 2025. It found nothing to suggest Incogni covers fewer than 420 brokers or misses its repeat-request schedule. Incogni published it. The report tests the company's own process, not how many of your listings disappear. Consumer Reports' 2024 test did not include Incogni. Custom removals (staff-handled requests for sites off the automated list) need the Unlimited plan.

    DeleteMe

    the established pick
    paid

    DeleteMe is the longest-running name in this category, run by Abine, Inc. since 2010. It adds human review to its automated sweeps: staff check broker listings instead of leaving it all to scripts. That human layer shows up in the price, which runs higher than Incogni's or EasyOptOuts'. The standard plan is $129 a year with quarterly reports. The $180 Premium plan adds six scans a year instead of four, 15 more broker sites, and more custom requests.

    Its list names 969 brokers, but some come only with higher plans such as Platinum and VIP, and others need a custom request. In Consumer Reports' August 2024 test of seven services, it was a midlevel performer, removing 27 percent of profiles after four months against 65 percent for EasyOptOuts.

    worth knowing

    Google Data Removal is free and narrower in scope. Google's "Results about you" tool lets you request removal of search results showing your address, phone number, or email. Since February 2026 in the US, it also covers government ID numbers (driver's license, passport, Social Security number) and alerts you to new results. It only changes Google Search, so the broker's own listing stays up. Use it alongside any paid service above.

    California residents get a free broker-removal tool. The state's DROP (Delete Request and Opt-out Platform) sends one deletion request to 600+ registered data brokers. Since 1 August 2026, brokers must check it at least every 45 days and delete matching data. Status updates can take up to 90 days.

    No removal service prevents new data from appearing. New brokers launch, and old ones copy public records again. In Consumer Reports' August 2024 test of seven services, every participant still appeared on some of 13 people-search sites after four months. Budget for this as a subscription.
