    Standard Setup

    last updated 2026-11-05 · 9 changes · a weekend, then slow migration · what changed

    Standard takes the same jobs further than Essentials. Your email, files, and notes move to services that cannot read them, and you own the address everything is tied to. Who this is for: you have decided that advertisers and data companies holding your whole life is itself the problem, beyond thieves and leaks. Expect one weekend of focus, then a few months of slow migration.

    builds on essentials This setup assumes Essentials is done: a password manager, a login-code app, Brave, Signal, encrypted DNS, and a free Proton Mail account. A card says builds on when it takes one of those further, and new when it adds a job Essentials did not cover.

    the setup

    Your own domain

    foundation
    ~30 minnew

    The job: A domain is an internet address you own, like yourname.com. Using it for your email means you can change email provider later and nobody has to learn a new address.

    Why this one: Owning the domain makes every later email choice free to change. Porkbun, Namecheap, and Gandi are common registrars (the companies that sell domains).

    Look for: A reputable registrar that hides your contact details from the public registry (called WHOIS privacy) and lets you transfer out.

    1. Buy a boring, easy-to-spell domain. You will say it out loud for years.
    2. Turn on WHOIS privacy. It is usually free and on by default now.
    3. Stop there for today. The next card connects it.

    Proton Mail on your domain

    email
    ~2 h, then months of driftbuilds on: your free Proton Mail account

    The job: Your main inbox moves to a provider that cannot read it, on an address you control. Your email is the key to every other account, so this is the move that matters most at this level.

    Why Proton Mail: Its paid plan lets you attach your own domain and bundles the alias service in the next card, so you stay in one account.

    Look for: End-to-end encryption, support for your own domain, and a way to export your mail. Backup: Mailbox.org if you want to use any mail app through IMAP (a standard that lets any app connect).

    1. Upgrade your free account, add your domain, and follow the DNS records it gives you. DNS records are a few lines you paste into your registrar.
    2. Make you@yourdomain your main address and forward your old inbox to it. Keep the old account open and let it become an empty hallway.
    3. This weekend, move the heavy hitters: banks, government, employer, and every account's recovery address. The long tail shows itself in forwarded mail over time.

    SimpleLogin

    email aliases
    ~15 minnew

    The job: An alias is a forwarding address you can switch off. Giving each company its own alias means a leak shows you who sold your address, and you can kill a spammer's address without touching your real one.

    Why SimpleLogin: It comes with the Proton plan from the last card, so there is no extra account to manage.

    Look for: Aliases that forward mail and let you reply, plus support for your own domain. Backup: Addy.io if you want an independent service.

    1. Turn on aliases in your Proton settings and install the browser extension.
    2. Follow one rule: people get your real address, companies get an alias. Do not retrofit old accounts. Switch them when they email you.
    3. Name each alias after the service in the note field as you create it. Without that, you will be afraid to delete any of them in six months.

    YubiKey ×2

    hardware keys
    ~1 hbuilds on: Ente Auth codes

    The job: A hardware key is a small device you tap to log in. It replaces typed codes on your most important accounts. A fake login page can trick you into typing a code, and it cannot trick a key.

    Why YubiKey: It is the most widely supported key. Buy two, because one lost key should never lock you out.

    Look for: Support for FIDO2 (the standard behind phishing-proof login), and the ability to register a spare. Backup: Nitrokey if open-source firmware matters to you.

    1. Buy two keys. Use one daily and keep one in a drawer.
    2. Register both on your email, password manager, and banking where supported. Then remove text-message login as a fallback.
    3. Store the spare somewhere other than your bag. It only helps if it exists when the main key doesn't.

    Proton VPN

    VPN
    ~20 minbuilds on: AdGuard DNS

    The job: A VPN hides what you do online from your internet provider and from public Wi-Fi. It moves that trust to the VPN company, so choose one that has been audited and promises not to keep logs.

    Why Proton VPN: It is audited, fast, and it bundles with the Proton account you already have, so you pay and sign in once.

    Look for: Independent audits, a clear no-logs policy, and a kill switch (it cuts your internet if the VPN drops). Backup: Windscribe, the closest runner-up to Proton on everyday ease of use, if you'd rather not tie your VPN to your Proton account.

    1. Add it to your existing Proton plan and install it on your phone and computer.
    2. Turn on the kill switch and NetShield, Proton's built-in ad and tracker blocking.
    3. Set it to connect automatically on untrusted Wi-Fi. Make it always on if speeds hold up for you.

    Filen

    cloud storage
    ~45 minnew

    The job: Cloud storage keeps your photos and files safe if a device dies. An encrypted one replaces a cloud the company can read.

    Why Filen: Even Filen cannot read what you store, and it has proper apps on every system, Linux included.

    Look for: Encryption where only you hold the key, apps for each of your devices, and a way to export everything. Backup: Tresorit if you want a polished, long-audited option and don't mind paying more for it.

    1. Create the account, turn on camera upload on your phone, and let it run overnight.
    2. Start your old provider's export now (for Google, that is Takeout). Archives take days, and the photo archive is the one people regret losing.
    3. Move the documents you use. Leave the rest until you need them.

    An offline backup

    backup
    ~1 h, then monthlynew

    The job: A backup is a second copy that your sync cannot overwrite. Sync copies a mistake or a deletion to every device, and a backup lets you go back.

    Why this one: An external drive with restic or BorgBackup (free tools that keep encrypted, dated copies) costs little and works with any cloud.

    Look for: Versioned copies (old versions are kept), encryption, and a copy that is not always plugged in.

    1. Buy an external drive and set the tool to back up your important folders.
    2. Run it monthly and keep the drive unplugged in between.
    3. Restore one file to prove it works. A backup you have never restored is a hope.

    Notesnook

    notes
    ~20 minnew

    The job: Your notes read like a diary of your life. An encrypted notes app replaces one where the company can read every page.

    Why Notesnook: It encrypts your notes end to end, runs on every platform, and offers a real export if you ever want to leave.

    Look for: End-to-end encryption, an app lock, and a plain export. Backup: Obsidian if you want your notes as plain files on your own device.

    1. Create an account and install the app on your phone and computer.
    2. Import from your old notes app if it offers a plain export. A messy import beats losing years of notes.
    3. Turn on the app lock, and send new notes here first. Move old ones as you need them.

    EasyOptOuts

    data brokers
    ~20 min, then ongoingnew

    The job: Data brokers are sites that collect and resell your name, address, and phone number. A removal service asks them to delete you, then keeps checking, because they re-list you over time.

    Why EasyOptOuts: It keeps removing you on a schedule, and a locked-down inbox matters little if anyone can still buy your home address for a few dollars.

    Look for: Ongoing re-removal, a clear list of the sites it covers, and service where you live. EasyOptOuts serves US residents only. Backup: Incogni or DeleteMe.

    1. Sign up and submit the details it needs to find your listings.
    2. Let the first sweep run. It is the slowest, and removals land over weeks.
    3. Leave monitoring on, and check the dashboard after the first month.

    after setup

    The email move is a season, and it has no single day. Three months of forwarded mail will surface accounts you forgot existed. For each one, log in and change the address or alias it. The day forwarding goes quiet is the day you have actually left.

    Keep your old email account. Empty it and secure it with a key. Old recovery paths and forgotten services still point there, and an account you control is safer than one someone else recycles.

    Going further: if a specific person or group is part of your threat, or you want your phone itself out of the big companies' hands, the Hardened setup is next. For most people, this level is the destination. Maintain it and live your life.
