    Recommended Setups

    last updated 2026-11-05 · 3 setups · what changed

    Each setup is a complete answer. It tells you what to use for each job, and why, so you don't have to compare anything. Pick the level that matches how much protection you need. Every pick links to its category page, for the day you want to look at alternatives.

    how to choose If you haven't read threat modeling, that's the real first step. The short version: most people belong on Essentials or Standard. Hardened is for people with a concrete reason to be there. It charges real daily friction for protection most people don't need.

    Why this one

    The single reason we picked it. Prices, versions, and settings live on the category page, so they stay current in one place.

    Look for, backup, and the catch

    The qualities any replacement should have. Where a runner-up makes sense, the backup line names it: go with the pick, and use the backup if it gives you trouble. Where the pick has a known weakness, a catch line says so.

    how the levels relate

    Each level takes the same jobs further. Blocking, for example, starts with Brave and encrypted DNS, then adds a VPN with its own blocker, then adds Tor. The domain you own in Standard becomes your everyday compartment in Hardened.

    Cards say what they build on or replace. The foundations stay. When a later level swaps something out, such as Mullvad in place of Proton VPN, the card says so.

    You can live between levels. Plenty of people run Standard plus GrapheneOS, or Essentials plus a VPN. The levels are coherent starting points, and nobody checks your membership.

    Done beats perfect. A finished Essentials setup protects you more than a Hardened setup you got 30% through and abandoned in week two.
